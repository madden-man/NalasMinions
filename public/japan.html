Dawn starts can dip toward 37–40°F (3–4 °C); the passes are cooler still. The Kii Peninsula is Japan's wettest region, and though November is its drier season, plan on at least one rainy stage. Shaded, humid trail: you sweat on the climbs and chill fast at stops.
👕 What to wear
  • Layering system: wicking base layer → fleece or light puffy → waterproof/breathable shell (rain and wind). No cotton.
  • Legs & feet: hiking pants (not shorts: mud, branches), wool socks, broken-in boots, gaiters optional.
  • Dawn starts (esp. Nov 14): beanie + light gloves + headlamp; stash them once the sun is up.
  • Rain: pack cover or dry bag for the daypack; spare socks.
  • Evenings: minshuku are casual and lend yukata, but old wooden houses get cold: warm sleep layer + thick socks.
  • Onsen (Yunomine, Tsuboyu, Bōki-dō): no swimsuit; bring a small towel.

Kyoto sits in a basin: crisp, often clear mornings (Fushimi Inari at dawn ≈ 45°F) that warm nicely by lunch. Mostly dry. Meoto Iwa is windy at the shore.
👕 What to wear
  • Proper fall wardrobe: sweater or fleece + a medium jacket or wool coat, scarf. Not overkill for dawn temples and evening light-ups.
  • Midday: layer down to a long sleeve; 10–15 km walking days, so closed shoes with cushion for gravel and stairs.
  • Nicer notch: one smart-casual outfit for Park Hyatt, kaiseki, and the tea ceremony (no dress code, but you'll want it).
  • Socks matter: shoes come off at temples, the tea house, and Tōdai-ji halls. Hole-free, easy on/off shoes.
  • Nara: deer are pushy; skip loose dangling bags with snacks.
🍁 Foliage timing: peak is forecast Nov 25 – Dec 7, so Nov 15–19 colors will be just turning. The famous Kiyomizu-dera night illumination starts Nov 22 — after you leave. Kōdai-ji (5-min walk from Park Hyatt) and Eikandō run mid-Nov light-ups that may already be on — verify and grab an evening.

⛑️ Kumano Kodo Safety

Five days on mountain trail with patchy signal, short daylight, and no shops between villages. The real risks are ordinary ones: a slip on wet stone, a turned ankle, getting caught out after dark. Everything here is about making those boring.

🚨 Emergency card — screenshot this and keep a paper copy in each daypack
  • 119 — ambulance / fire / mountain rescue. Say "Eigo, onegai shimasu" (English, please); interpreters cover English. Free from any phone.
  • 110 — police.
  • 050-3816-2787 — Japan Visitor Hotline (JNTO), 24 h, English. For anything non-urgent: a clinic, a lost passport, a closed road.
  • Where you are: the Nakahechi has numbered signposts about every 500 m. Read the dispatcher the number on the last post you passed, plus the stage ("between Koguchi and Nachi"). Drop a Google Maps pin too, if you have signal.
  • Lodges — tonight's host is your first call when you're late, hurt, or stuck. Their numbers are on the Kumano Travel voucher (DP200503); save all five in both phones.
  • Hospitals that take foreign travelers: Sakura Shinryojo Clinic, Hongū (0735-42-8101) · Shingu Municipal Medical Center (0735-31-3333) · Minami Wakayama Medical Center, Tanabe (0739-26-7050).
  • Insurance — policy number + 24 h assistance line on the card. Ask any clinic for a receipt and an English medical form for the claim.

🆘 If something goes wrong

  • Someone is hurt and can't walk: stop, get them warm (blanket, layers), note the signpost number, call 119. With no signal, one of you walks to the last place you had signal, but only in daylight, and the other stays put and visible.
  • Running late: call the lodge before 4 pm so they know. Don't race the dark on stone steps: headlamps on, slow down.
  • Lost the trail: stop and go back to the last signpost; the route is well marked, and a missing marker usually means a wrong turn.
  • Never split up on trail, even to go faster.
  • Heavy rain or a typhoon: skip the stage. Stone paving and roots are slick when wet, and that's where most injuries happen. Take the bus to tonight's lodge, and confirm with the host that the luggage shuttle is still running.
  • Earthquake on the coast (Kii-Tanabe, Kii-Katsuura): if the shaking is strong or long, go uphill at once, without waiting for a tsunami warning. Hotel Urashima is on the waterfront: on check-in, find its evacuation route.
  • Missed the last bus: lodges call taxis; keep ¥10k cash for the fare.

✅ Before you go

  • Install the Safety Tips app (Japan Tourism Agency): earthquake, tsunami and weather alerts in English.
  • Insurance must cover hiking and emergency evacuation (see the checklist).
  • Send the stage plan and lodge names to someone at home, and check in with them each night.
  • On Nov 9, check the JMA forecast and the Tanabe tourism bureau's trail notices for closures or detours.
  • Screenshot the bus times for Nov 10 (Kii-Tanabe → Takijiri) and Nov 12 (Chikatsuyu → Hosshinmon-ōji); evening buses are few.
