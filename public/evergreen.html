Wildfire

Harden the house, know two ways out, leave at the Warning

The house survives on its first five feet and its roof; we survive on leaving early. Jeffco calls an Evacuation Warning, then an Order; with Nala we go at the Warning.

🛡️ Prevent

  • Zone 1: rock, nothing burnable
  • Soffit gaps sealed, vents mesh
  • Trees limbed; junipers out
  • Roof fixed; Class A when new

🎒 Prepare

  • LookoutAlert on both phones
  • Two routes, driven day and night
  • Go-bags and Nala by the door
  • N95s and a HEPA purifier

🚨 Respond

  • Warning = we leave, with Nala
  • Drive away from the smoke
  • Shelter: shut up, HVAC off
  • Check jeffcom911 active alerts

Where the help is: a free Evergreen Fire/Rescue home assessment sets the mitigation list; LookoutAlert sends the evacuation texts; chipper day and the Jeffco SLASH yards take the slash; Jeffco's resiliency code is the zone standard; AirNow tracks smoke.

Winter and outages

A week snowed in without power: the gas fireplace is the heat

The planning case is a March upslope storm with the power out for up to a week. Water keeps flowing (Evergreen Metro, no well), but the boiler needs electricity, so the gas fireplace and a power station are the heat and light.

🛡️ Prevent

  • Trees limbed off the lines
  • Traction tires by November 1
  • Frost-proof faucets, shutoffs
  • Thermostat 65°F when away

🎒 Prepare

  • Power station; boiler inlet
  • Fireplace lights unpowered
  • Week of water, 14 days food
  • Car kit: blankets, shovel

🚨 Respond

  • Drip far tap, open cabinets
  • Fridge 4 h, freezer 48 h shut
  • Generator 20 ft from windows
  • COtrip before any drive

Where the help is: COtrip for closures, Xcel or CORE outage maps and texts, the traction law, Jeffco snow plowing (residential roads last), and Ready.gov on outages for food safety.

Do it: ⚡ Power ❄️ Winter 🎒 Supplies 🏠 House

Floods and storms

Above the creek, inside at the thunder, cars under cover

Summer afternoons bring the fast hazards: Bear Creek flash floods, lightning, hail and the wind days that trigger power shutoffs. The fixes are mostly drainage, roof and habit.

🛡️ Prevent

  • Right side regraded from house
  • Dead trees down near the house
  • Gutters and culverts clear
  • Roof repaired before hail

🎒 Prepare

  • Week of bottled water
  • Weather radio; WEA alerts on
  • FEMA zone known
  • Insurance review each January

🚨 Respond

  • Never drive through water
  • Thunder: inside for 30 minutes
  • Hail: cars in the garage
  • Wind day: expect a shutoff

Where the help is: the FEMA flood map for the lot, NWS Boulder for warnings, COtrip for Hwy 74 and canyon closures, and the insurer's written risk score and discounts each January.

Do it: 🏠 House 📣 Alerts 🔥 Wildfire 🥾 Hiking

Home hazards

Alarms on every floor, the gas line fixed, Nala in at dusk

This house runs on natural gas (2019 boiler, gas fireplace, unvented gas range) with an attached garage, so carbon monoxide and gas are the home risks; the August inspection found the alarms missing and an exterior gas line corroding. Radon is a Jeffco given. Lions, bears and elk come through the yard.

🛡️ Prevent

  • Corroded gas line replaced
  • Boiler and fireplace serviced
  • Damper open when it burns
  • Radon test; mitigate over 4

🎒 Prepare

  • Smoke alarm in every bedroom
  • CO alarm upstairs, each floor
  • Extinguishers: kitchen, garage
  • Wrench hung at the gas meter

🚨 Respond

  • Gas smell: out, meter, call
  • CO alarm: everyone out, 911
  • Lion: stand big, back away
  • Bear in the trash: inside, CPW

Where the help is: Jeffco radon (half of local homes test high), the Colorado CO alarm law, CPW on bears and lions, and the inspection report's items 2, 3, 13 and 33.

Do it: 🛠️ Home hazards 🏠 House 🦌 Wildlife 🐾 Nala
🥾

Hiking

Dawn start, noon turnaround, a plan at home, a way to call

Four habits cover almost every trail emergency: start at dawn and turn around at noon, leave a trip plan, keep Nala leashed, and carry a way to call that does not need cell signal. Alpine Rescue Team is free and local, and should never be needed.

🛡️ Prevent

  • Off the summit by noon
  • Trip plan left at home
  • Leash on; no dawn or dusk
  • CAIC check for winter trips

🎒 Prepare

  • Ten Essentials in the pack
  • Satellite SOS or inReach
  • CORSAR card or Wild Pass
  • Nala: water, booties, kit

🚨 Respond

  • Thunder: down, below the trees
  • Lost: call 911, stay put
  • AMS: stop; worse: descend
  • Moose or lion: back away slow

Where the help is: CORSAR and the SAR program, Alpine Rescue Team, the CAIC avalanche forecast, Jeffco park closures (mud season), Mount Blue Sky timed entry, and hiking with a dog.

Do it: 🥾 Hiking 🐾 Nala 🎒 Supplies

📋 Grab sheet

The page for the fridge and the go-bags: what we do, what we carry, who we call.

🗺️ What we do

  • Leave at the Warning, not the Order.With Nala we are a Warning household; early beats the jam on Hwy 74.
  • Two routes on the fridge.Hwy 74 east through the canyon, Hwy 74 north to I-70 at El Rancho, Hwy 73 south to Conifer; never toward smoke.
  • Meeting points.End of the driveway for a house fire; a named spot in Golden or Lakewood when roads split us; one out-of-state contact, by text.
  • Grab the six P's.People and Nala, prescriptions, papers, personal needs, priceless things, phones and chargers.
  • Shelter in place.Windows shut, HVAC off, purifier on, Nala inside, cars loaded anyway.
  • Snowed in without power.Gas fireplace room, taps dripping, fridge shut, check on neighbors on day two.
